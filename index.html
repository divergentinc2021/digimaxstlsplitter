Walls & hollowing solid

Off = parts are saved solid and your slicer sets perimeters and infill (usual for FDM — 3–4 walls, 15–20 % infill is plenty for a base this thick). On = the tile is saved as a shell of this wall thickness: far less material, for pouring filler, resin / SLS, or when the slicer must see a true shell. Adds a few seconds per tile; the outer surface is untouched.

Dowel holes on cut faces off

Holes are only placed where both sides are solid. Not needed if you glue with PU.

View & print simulation

Each layer is sliced from the real tile: the head traces the perimeters at the line width, then straight infill at the chosen density, alternating 45° per layer. A preview of the slicer's work, not its output. The time estimate samples these settings across the real slices and ignores acceleration and cooling — expect ±30 %.

Repaired source

The loaded model re-exported as one closed solid: welded, zero-area triangles dropped, base on z = 0, at the size shown above.

Why this works when other splitters don't: tiles are cut with Manifold booleans, so cut faces are always closed — then every tile is independently audited and the volumes must add up before you can download. Source (MIT)
